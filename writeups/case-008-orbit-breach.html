Executive Summary A single packet capture from Cairo Ground Station's HoneySat-NET-01 was reconstructed end to end. Three concurrent VPN logins were observed, one legitimate and two malicious. One of the malicious sessions belonged to the attacker, who beaconed to external C2 infrastructure over TLS, then pivoted via SSH into the satellite TT&C environment using an anomalous service account. From there, a full satellite command sequence was executed — ending in disabled transmission authentication, exfiltration of a 249,856-byte navigation data bundle, and anti-forensics cleanup. The second malicious session was identified as a fully separate decoy chain targeting a decoy satellite asset, distinguished from the real intrusion by its JA3 TLS fingerprint.

Scenario

Night shift at Cairo Ground Station was supposed to be routine. By dawn, operators reported irregular behavior on orbital support systems, while automated monitoring insisted most traffic was normal.

A single network capture from HoneySat-NET-01 was provided for investigation — containing telemetry chatter, operator access, scheduled sync jobs, and unknown external activity woven into the same timeline. The objective was to reconstruct what actually happened directly from packet evidence.

Key Findings

  • Three concurrent VPN logins were observed; one legitimate (198.18.5.44) was ruled out early, leaving two to investigate further — the attacker session (185.193.88.31, SES-VPN771A) and what was later confirmed as a decoy session.
  • A C2 beacon to api.orbit-sync.com was identified via TLS SNI on the legitimate vendor VPN's outbound path.
  • The attacker pivoted via SSH into the TT&C uplink host and authenticated as svc-ephem from an IP inconsistent with that account's legitimate usage pattern.
  • A full satellite command sequence was reconstructed: status check → rejected thruster test → forced safe mode → audit sink nulled → transmission auth disabled → navigation bundle exported.
  • 249,856 bytes of navigation data were confirmed transferred to 104.244.77.12:9443 across three packet chunks.
  • Anti-forensics commands (alert queue clear, log rotation) were executed immediately after exfiltration completed.
  • A second, parallel VPN session was traced to a fully separate decoy chain targeting a decoy satellite asset, confirmed distinct via JA3 fingerprint comparison.
  • Two additional artifacts — a large internal-destination transfer and a late external SYN — were investigated and ruled out as unrelated to the primary intrusion.

Decoy Session Analysis

A second malicious VPN session running alongside the attacker's session was investigated to determine whether it was related activity. Its traffic led through a separate internal pivot host (10.77.0.7) to a different TT&C target host (172.21.9.9), reaching a decoy satellite asset entirely — not the real target of the intrusion.

Analyst Notes

Started by filtering udp.port == 1194 and found three concurrent VPN logins; ruled out the legitimate one first, then had two remaining sessions to trace independently.
Filtered on the VPN host's IP and tcp.port == 443 to identify the outbound C2 beacon and its TLS SNI.
Followed the SSH session into the TT&C environment and cross-referenced the authenticating account against known legitimate svc-ephem usage to flag it as anomalous.
Reconstructed the full command sequence by following the SES-5700 TCP stream end to end, rather than relying on isolated packets.
Verified actual data transfer volume against the export command by tracing the subsequent data-transfer packets, rather than assuming export success.
Investigated two additional artifacts (a large internal transfer and a late external SYN) and ruled both out based on destination and timing rather than dismissing or including them without verification.
Compared JA3 fingerprints between the two parallel C2 channels to confirm the second session was an unrelated decoy chain, not a second stage of the same intrusion.
